# Q2 People & Talent Brief

## Executive Summary

The second quarter people and talent review shows a business that is hiring with more discipline, retaining more of its critical talent, and building stronger management habits across the organisation. Hiring volume is lower than the same period last year, but the quality of shortlisted candidates has improved and time spent by hiring managers in final interview loops has been reduced. The operating assumption for the quarter is that every headcount decision must be justified against delivery impact, team health, and manager capacity to onboard well.

Across the business, leaders are asking for a clearer view of where the talent system is supporting execution and where it is introducing drag. The most consistent themes are slow final approvals for specialist roles, uneven quality in candidate briefing packs, and a need for tighter follow through once offers are accepted. At the same time, managers report better engagement from employees when team priorities are described in plain language and linked directly to the company plan.

This brief summarises the current state of headcount, hiring pipeline performance, retention signals, leadership development progress, and decisions that will likely need executive attention before the end of the quarter. It is intended to serve as a realistic document for ingestion, retrieval, and provenance validation in the Chief of Staff platform.

## Headcount And Hiring Pipeline

Approved hiring for the quarter remains focused on engineering, product, data, and a small number of commercial leadership roles. Engineering continues to account for the largest share of open positions, with demand strongest in platform reliability, security, and internal tooling. Product hiring is more selective and is primarily concentrated on senior individual contributors who can operate across discovery, delivery, and stakeholder management.

The recruiting team reports that the top of funnel remains healthy for mid level roles, but more constrained for senior technical talent. Candidate response rates improved after interview panels were shortened from five rounds to four and when hiring managers included a concise role narrative in outreach. Roles with the strongest conversion share three characteristics: clear business context, visible executive sponsorship, and compensation ranges communicated earlier in the process.

Offer acceptance remains positive overall, but there is a small cluster of declines in infrastructure and analytics where external packages have moved faster than internal review cycles. Recruiters are now escalating compensation exceptions earlier so that final approval does not become the main reason a candidate disengages. A weekly review of open offers has been introduced to reduce lag between verbal acceptance and contract signature.

## Retention And Engagement

Retention indicators are stable, though there are meaningful differences between functions. Voluntary attrition is lowest in finance and operations, moderate in product, and highest in data engineering where external demand remains strong. Exit interviews continue to point to career progression clarity and manager quality as the most important drivers of whether high performers stay through periods of change.

Pulse survey comments from the quarter show employees want fewer organisational messages and more local clarity. Teams with the highest engagement scores tend to have managers who explain tradeoffs, repeat priorities consistently, and close the loop after decisions are made. Employees are notably more positive when they understand why a project was paused than when it simply disappears from planning conversations.

There is also evidence that manager attention matters more than programme volume. Teams that participate in fewer formal initiatives but hold regular one to ones and quarterly growth conversations are showing stronger retention than teams with more central interventions but weaker direct management habits. This suggests the next phase of people operations should emphasise manager enablement over adding another broad engagement campaign.

## Leadership Development

The leadership development cohort is progressing well, with strong attendance and useful feedback from both first line managers and more experienced leaders. Participants say the most valuable sessions have been those that translate abstract leadership principles into repeatable operating practices: writing clearer updates, handling performance concerns earlier, and making decisions with explicit assumptions.

Managers in the programme are also asking for more support on organisational design basics. They want practical guidance on shaping team charters, defining role boundaries, and deciding when a coordination problem requires process rather than another meeting. Several leaders noted that their teams become more effective when the manager uses a written brief before making cross functional requests.

The learning and development team recommends continuing the programme into the next quarter with a lighter but more applied format. Proposed additions include peer case reviews, simple templates for decision logs, and a short module on running effective quarterly planning conversations. These changes should improve transfer from workshop discussion into day to day management behaviour.

## Upcoming Decisions

Three decisions are likely to require executive input before the quarter closes. First, the company needs a clear rule for compensation exception handling on hard to hire specialist roles. Second, there should be agreement on whether the data engineering retention risk justifies targeted market adjustments or non cash retention measures. Third, the leadership development programme needs a named executive sponsor if it is to move from a useful pilot into a durable management system.

From an operating perspective, the people team is in a stronger position than it was at the start of the year. Core rhythms are improving, managers have better signals, and the organisation is more willing to discuss tradeoffs directly. The remaining challenge is consistency: the system works best when leaders use the same few practices well, rather than introducing extra initiatives that create noise without improving execution.
